## 02 — What belongs in a whole-home audio plan?

Whole-home audio connects selected rooms so music can play independently or together. A media room adds a display and a front soundstage built around the seats, with surround and height speakers when the room and household justify them. DAVG helps choose the right speakers, TV and control path for each space, then makes everyday use consistent across the house.

**Three valid starting points:** music in a few rooms; music across many rooms with separate zones; or a family room where TV, music, lighting and shades should work together. A dedicated cinema is a different room brief, linked below.

## 03 — Listening and viewing moments

**Choose the moment before the hardware:**

| Moment | What the room needs | What can be excessive |
|---|---|---|
| Dinner with conversation | Even, low-level coverage and simple volume/grouping | A single loud source at one end of an open plan |
| Movie night with family | Visible dialogue anchor, appropriate bass, screen readable from the seats | Calling ceiling music speakers a complete surround system |
| Focused music listening | Proper left/right image and a defined listening position | Uniform ceiling coverage in place of a stereo soundstage |
| A game on multiple screens | Source selection, TV size and viewing angles by seat | Paying for whole-home video distribution where local sources suffice |
| Patio and kitchen together | Independent outdoor volume with optional indoor grouping | Outdoor speakers tied to the kitchen volume without a boundary plan |

The brief should list the people, seats, daylight, neighboring rooms, favorite sources and desired controls. That list determines the system; the equipment follows.

## 04 — What connects to what?

**Music path:** music service or local source → supported player/source → local or distributed amplification → speaker cable → speakers in each designed zone. Zones can select their own source and volume or form a supported group. A single music zone is not the same as one physical speaker.

**TV path:** streaming box, TV app, cable box or game console → TV and an appropriate audio return or switching path → soundbar, AVR/processor or amplification → front, surround, subwoofer and optional height channels. The exact HDMI/eARC, surround formats and source behavior are confirmed for the selected products; plugging everything into a network switch does not create a video or speaker signal path.

**Control layer:** Control4 can expose supported sources, select Watch or Listen, coordinate room power and volume, group supported music rooms, and trigger installed lighting or shade scenes. It does not replace the television, decoder/amplifier, subscriptions or a source's own licensing. Verify each TV/source driver and recovery behavior before promising a one-button result. [Control4 Watch and Listen guidance](https://www.control4.com/help/c4/user/userguide/content/topics/entertainment/basics-watch.htm), [multi-room audio](https://www.control4.com/solutions/audio).

## 05 — Multi-zone sound map

## 06 — Which speaker type, and why Sonance or Triad?

Start with the sound the room must deliver, then choose how visible the speaker is allowed to be. **Sonance** is a strong architectural-audio family for whole-home music and discreet integrations; the current [Visual Experience (VX) series](https://sonance.com/collections/visual-experience), [Invisible Series](https://sonance.com/collections/invisible-series), and selected [Single Stereo products](https://sonance.com/collections/visual-experience-series-sst-in-ceiling) illustrate different visibility and room-size choices. Older Visual Performance (VP) products exist, but do not present VP as the default current lineup without confirming the exact available model. **Triad** offers [distributed audio, in-wall/in-room front speakers, center channels, soundbars and subwoofers](https://www.triadspeakers.com/the-triad-collection); its [amplification and matrix paths](https://www.control4.com/solutions/products/amplifiers-and-receivers) may serve multi-room systems. These are product-family comparisons, not claims that DAVG installed or stocks every model.

| Approach | What it does for the homeowner | Good fit | Limit / tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sonance VX in-ceiling or in-wall | Architectural music coverage with a visible, intentional grille | Kitchens, dining and general multi-room zones | Placement and model/trim matter more than making a uniform ceiling grid. |
| Sonance Invisible | Keeps the finished wall/ceiling plane free of visible grilles | Design-led rooms where the finish can be engineered and serviced | Finish, substrate, access and repair are part of the installed system; not an automatic best speaker for every room. |
| Sonance Single Stereo | Carries left and right program material through one appropriate speaker model | Small or awkward spaces where a conventional stereo pair does not fit | One physical opening is not equivalent to a widely spaced stereo pair at a focused seat. |
| Triad in-wall/in-room LCR and center | Establishes a front image and anchored dialogue around the TV | Family media rooms and higher-output screen rooms | Speaker positions and furniture/sightlines control the result; ceiling music speakers are not a substitute. |
| Triad soundbar or front-channel solution | Improves a TV's dialogue and can be visually matched to the display | Finished rooms with limited wall access | A front bar alone does not create discrete rear surround or overhead height channels. |
| Triad subs and distributed components | Add low-frequency support or route music to multiple zones when selected appropriately | Rooms that need bass or a coherent central audio path | Confirm amplifier loads, sub placement, source compatibility and actual room measurements. |

**Wired or wireless?** A wired passive speaker uses appropriately specified speaker cable and an amplifier; a standalone wireless/active speaker still needs power and a supported network/control path. A finished home may warrant an intentional hybrid. Choose the approach from concealment, installation access, grouping, service and budget—not from a rule that one connection type is always superior.

**Photo sequence:** genuine, permissioned close-ups of a Sonance VX grille, an installed Invisible condition and a Triad front speaker/soundbar in a real room. A product image must say “manufacturer product image” if not a DAVG installation. Each caption answers *why choose this* and *what is the compromise*. Never show a speaker family as part of a DAVG project without checking its record.

## 07 — How many speakers per room?

These are **starting configurations to compare**, not placement specifications or promised quantities. The answer changes with area, ceiling height, wall openings, seating, speaker dispersion, volume target, adjacent zones and whether the space serves music or TV.

| Room and goal | First configuration to examine | What changes the count or placement |
|---|---|---|
| Small bath, hall or narrow secondary room | One suitable Single Stereo model, or two speakers when a stereo pair fits | Room geometry, moisture rating, available cavity and desired stereo image. |
| Kitchen or dining music zone | Two architectural speakers as a first stereo pair | Long islands, separate dining seating, noisy cooking areas or coverage gaps may lead to more positions. |
| Large open kitchen/living plan | Four or more distributed speaker positions **as a study**, potentially divided into separately controlled zones | Need for independent kitchen/living volume and the risk of audio spilling into quieter rooms. |
| Library or dedicated music seat | Two properly placed left/right speakers; consider a sub for bass where useful | Listening distance, acoustics, visible-speaker preference and desired playback level. |
| Everyday TV room | Front left, center and right plus subwoofer (3.1) is a meaningful dialogue-first path; compare 5.1 if surround seats and routes allow | A quality, well-positioned front stage may beat a poorly placed larger channel count. |
| Flexible media room with Atmos | Study 5.1.2 or 5.1.4 after seating and layout are set | Physical surrounds, ceiling structure, decoder/amplifier, content and room boundaries. See §09. |
| Patio | Begin with listening areas and neighbor-facing boundary, then examine two or more weather-appropriate positions | Shape, exposure, subwoofer need, independent volume and permitted cable routes. |

For each room, show on the proposal: **physical speaker count; number of listening zones; number of TV audio channels; subwoofers; chosen source(s); how the room is controlled.** These are different numbers. Ask DAVG to draw the proposed locations on a floor plan and explain the benefit of every additional position. Outdoor design belongs in the [Outdoor Entertainment guide](/solutions/outdoor-entertainment/).

## 08 — Which TV belongs in this room?

**Section job:** compare display technologies in the actual light and use of the room. This replaces the former wall X-ray module. Show one room/seat photograph and a clear side-by-side comparison; a technical finish cutaway is optional lower down only if it changes mounting cost or screen choice.

| Screen path | What it is | Strong reason to choose it | What to check before buying |
|---|---|---|---|
| OLED | Self-emitting pixels that can turn off individually | Deep blacks, high contrast and broad off-axis viewing for film-first rooms | Direct reflections and actual model brightness; static-logo or all-day news/signage habits and manufacturer care guidance. |
| Mini LED LCD | LCD with many small backlight LEDs and local dimming | Bright living rooms, large screens and varied daytime viewing | Model-specific glare handling, dimming/blooming, viewing angles and black-level behavior. |
| Standard LED/QLED LCD | LCD with LED backlight; “QLED” usually describes a quantum-dot color approach, not self-emitting pixels | A useful value or secondary-room choice when the chosen model performs well | Backlight type and local dimming; a QLED label alone does not guarantee Mini LED contrast. |
| Art / Frame-style TV | A lifestyle TV designed to resemble framed art; panel tech depends on the model | Living spaces where the screen's appearance when off matters most | Actual picture performance, matte finish, bezel, outlet/connection-box route, art service and mounting detail; do not assume every Frame or Frame Pro has the same panel. |

**4K versus 8K:** 4K UHD is 3840 × 2160 pixels; 8K UHD is 7680 × 4320, four times the pixels. More pixels alone do not solve glare, poor contrast or a screen too small for the seating distance. Most 8K viewing relies on upscaling lower-resolution material; verify *native* 8K sources you actually use before paying primarily for resolution. The default shortlist for a typical family/media room should compare strong 4K models at the right size and light performance before an 8K upgrade. [Samsung on resolution and upscaling](https://www.samsung.com/us/tvs/tv-buying-guide/what-are-8k-tvs/).

**Selection sequence:** measure nearest/farthest seats and viewing angles → observe daytime reflections with shades open and closed → decide whether art appearance or movie picture is the higher priority → compare appropriate screen sizes in the actual wall elevation → verify HDR formats, game features, connections, control integration and service access on the *exact model*. A large screen above a fireplace is not automatically a good eye-level TV. [Sony mounting and sightline guidance](https://www.sony.com/electronics/support/articles/00124300); [LG panel comparison](https://www.lg.com/us/tvs/tv-buying-guide/picture-quality-tv-guide); [Samsung The Frame family](https://www.samsung.com/us/lifestyle-tvs/the-frame/highlights/); [Sony OLED image-retention guidance](https://www.sony.com/electronics/support/articles/00173479).

## 09 — What does Dolby Atmos change?

Dolby Atmos carries audio objects and height information that a compatible playback system renders around the listening position. A pair of ceiling speakers used for background music does **not** by itself make a room an Atmos room. A successful installation also needs an Atmos source/title, compatible processing and amplification, a suitable speaker layout and tested signal path. [Dolby speaker setup](https://www.dolby.com/about/support/guide/dolby-atmos-speaker-setup/).

| Layout | Count and actual role | Good reason to select it | Expectation to set |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 / 2.1 | Left and right; optional sub | Focused music or a simple TV sound improvement | No dedicated center dialogue or surround field. |
| 3.1 | Front left, center, right plus sub | Dialogue-first family TV where rear speakers are impractical | Strong front presentation; no discrete rear surround or overhead height. |
| 5.1 | Five main speakers (front L/C/R and two surrounds) plus sub | A real surround field without a height layer | Surround is not the same thing as Atmos height playback. |
| 5.1.2 | Five main speakers, one sub, two overhead/height speakers | Flexible room that can accommodate correctly placed surrounds and a height pair | The “.2” means two height speakers, **not** two more subwoofers. |
| 5.1.4 | Five main speakers, one sub, four overhead/height speakers | Room and seats that benefit from a more complete height field | More installation and processor channels; placement beats channel count. |

An Atmos-capable soundbar may be a valid lower-construction option, with some models using virtualization or upward-firing drivers. It should be described honestly rather than equated to discrete overhead loudspeakers. Base-layer speakers must anchor dialogue and effects around the screen and seats; ceiling units serve the height layer when that is the design. Ask DAVG to show a plan for the center speaker, surrounds, height positions, subwoofer(s), listening positions and room openings—not merely a “Dolby Atmos” logo. [Dolby 5.1.2 placement guide](https://www.dolby.com/about/support/guide/speaker-setup-guides/5.1.2-overhead-speaker-setup-guide/).

**Media room or private cinema?** Choose a media room when family use, daylight, conversation or flexible furnishings stay important; it can still have a serious screen and 5.1.2/5.1.4 layout. Choose a [Private Cinema](/solutions/private-cinemas/) when the room can prioritize fixed seating, light control, acoustics, isolation and dedicated movie viewing. Neither decision follows from the number of speakers alone.

## 10 — What does Control4 do for TV and music?

**Scenario 1 — Watch TV:** select Watch on a Halo remote or supported interface → choose the desired source → turn on the assigned display and audio path → control the correct volume and transport. If the lighting/shading system is installed and integrated, a separately specified scene can dim light or adjust shades. The design must also show what the family presses when a source app signs out, updates or stops responding. [Control4 entertainment](https://www.control4.com/solutions/video-and-home-theater), [Halo remotes](https://www.control4.com/solutions/products/remotes).

**Scenario 2 — Play music:** select Listen in the kitchen → start an available service/source → add the dining room and patio when desired → adjust each room's volume independently → remove the patio or end the session without stopping another room. The chosen source, account, subscription, music-routing hardware and driver support determine what is possible. [Control4 multi-room audio](https://www.control4.com/solutions/audio), [Listen and room grouping](https://www.control4.com/help/c4/user/userguide/content/topics/entertainment/basics-listen.htm).

**Scenario 3 — Movie to normal evening:** leave the media room on its own TV source; stop its Watch activity when finished; music elsewhere remains a separate session unless explicitly linked. If lighting/shades belong to the automation scope, define what returns to normal. A single Control4 interface can simplify the routine, but it is **control and orchestration**, not a promise that every app, TV or music service is supported forever.

**Demonstration to request before sign-off:** show Watch, Listen, group/ungroup, independent room volume, power-off, manual recovery and what happens to the room if internet or a streaming service is unavailable. Confirm the actual TV, streamer, AVR, Triad/other amplifier, Lutron system and Control4 driver combination in the signed equipment schedule.

## 12 — What changes the investment?

| Choice | What adds scope | What the proposal must separate |
|---|---|---|
| Two music rooms vs many zones | Additional speaker positions, amplifier channels, grouping and cable paths | Number of zones **and** number of physical speakers; independent sources and controls. |
| Visible grille vs plaster-over speaker | Finish trade coordination, cavity/assembly verification, repair complexity | Product, substrate/finish labor, protection and final tuning. |
| Soundbar/3.1 vs 5.1.2/5.1.4 | Surround/height locations, AVR/processor channels, subwoofer, calibration | Speaker/channel schedule and precise TV/audio signal path. |
| OLED/Mini LED/Frame vs a basic TV | Screen, mount, reflection control, art/connection-box decisions and source compatibility | Exact model and size, bracket, location, in-wall or remote connections, and care. |
| New build vs finished home | Open-wall routes differ from fishing or repairing finished surfaces | Access assumptions, electrical/millwork work and change allowances. |
| Standalone controls vs Control4 integration | Source programming, scenes, driver validation and household training | Supported devices, programming, user interfaces and ongoing service assumptions. |

Show per-room intended result and hardware path; do not hide fundamentally different rooms in a single “whole-home audio” price. Public installed bands require DAVG estimates with scope and date. A homeowner should be able to remove a room or adjust a speaker/display tier and understand the consequence.

## 13 — Build route, testing and handoff

**New construction:** finalize room use, speaker/TV positions, routes and power before rough-in; verify construction drawings before finishes. **Renovation:** coordinate with millwork, fireplaces, ceilings, lighting and shade locations while access is available. **Retrofit:** survey existing cable and accessible paths; show where visible repair may be needed and where a wireless/active product avoids it. **Upgrade/takeover:** test existing speakers, cable, amplification, network and control before discarding them.

The recurring failures are specific: too few speakers to cover a large area at conversation volume; ceiling music speakers passed off as TV surrounds; center speaker hidden in the wrong place; display selected without daylight and seating; AVR/HDMI/streamer mismatch; Control4 activities that work only when one app stays signed in. Each should have a drawing, model check or test that prevents it. At handoff, demonstrate each room's sources, zone grouping, volume, Watch/Listen behavior, calibrated TV audio, family recovery steps and labeled documentation.

## 14 — Denver rooms and late questions

Denver-area homes often combine bright glazing, reflective hard surfaces, open plans, finished basements and indoor/outdoor entertaining. They change screen glare, acoustic boundaries, cable access and speaker placement. Link first to [Denver-area services](/areas-served/) and only add a location-specific example where an approved project or measured condition supports it.

**Can I keep a Frame TV in the living room and a better movie TV elsewhere?** Yes. A design-led art TV in the visible living area and a picture-first screen in a family/media room may be more honest than forcing one display to solve both briefs. See §08.

**Do I need four ceiling speakers for Atmos?** No. A supported 5.1.2 layout uses two height speakers; 5.1.4 uses four. Neither works as intended without the correct base layer, source and processor. See §09 and Dolby's layout guides.

**Does one zone mean one speaker?** No. A zone is an independently controlled listening area; it may contain one suitable Single Stereo speaker, a stereo pair or several distributed speakers. Count both in the room schedule. See §07.

**Can a finished home join whole-home music without opening every wall?** Often a hybrid is possible: reuse verified existing cable, add targeted runs in accessible areas and use appropriate powered/wireless products in selected spaces. Compatibility, power, grouping and appearance are checked before proposing that mix. See §§06 and 13.

**Will Control4 replace streaming subscriptions or make every TV app work the same way?** No. It controls supported devices and activities; subscription rights, app updates, available services and driver behavior remain separate. See §10.

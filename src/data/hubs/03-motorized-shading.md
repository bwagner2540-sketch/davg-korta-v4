## 02 — What must each shade accomplish?

Motorized shading is an architectural system, not an accessory selected after the room is complete. Fabric, openness, motor, power, pocket, fascia, bracket, control, and window geometry have to work together to manage daylight without compromising the intended interior.

DAVG coordinates those details with the lighting and control architecture so shades can operate individually, by room, on schedule, or as part of a scene.

## 03 — Light, privacy and the view

Morning light can enter gradually. West-facing glass can respond before glare overtakes the room. A cinema or media scene can lower the appropriate shades with the lights. Bedrooms can gain privacy without adding another visible device or daily task.

When shading is right, the movement is quiet, aligned, and expected. The window remains the focus.

## 04 — Fabric, hardware, power and control

The design combines window dimensions, orientation, solar exposure, fabric performance, roll direction, hembar alignment, motor selection, power, structural blocking, pockets or fascia, control groups, keypad functions, sensors, and automation logic.

Large or repeated openings require elevation-level coordination so adjacent shades align visually and move in a deliberate sequence.

## 06 — Choose fabric, form and Lutron family

**Build this as the main homeowner selection guide.** The order matters: **goal at each window → fabric/privacy → treatment form → visible/concealed design → exact shade family and feasible dimensions → power/control → construction and investment**. Give one short recommendation at the end of each step. A person who arrives from a “Lutron shades near me” search should not have to open a manufacturer site to understand which family is likely to suit their home.

### Step 1 — What must the window do?

| Priority | Best starting point | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Keep a view and reduce daytime glare | Solar-screen roller fabric sampled against that orientation; consider 1%, 3%, 5% or 10% openness | More openness usually preserves more view but admits more direct light; an open-weave fabric is not dependable nighttime privacy when the room is brighter than outdoors |
| Daytime glow with more privacy | Translucent/privacy fabric | It diffuses light but sacrifices the clear view; inspect it from outside after dark |
| Bedroom darkness | Opaque/blackout fabric, with side channels, bottom/sill and pocket detail if strong light exclusion matters | Blackout fabric alone does not eliminate edge gaps; distinguish room-darkening from light-tight construction |
| Both daytime view and night privacy/darkness | Two separately controlled layers, when there is adequate mounting space, or a solar roller paired with drapery | Dual fabric requires more depth, more drives/controls and careful sequence; Palladiom and Triathlon do not have the same dual-bracket options as Sivoia |
| Large view wall or specialty opening | Elevation-level shade schedule and engineered drive/fabric review | Width, drop, aspect ratio, roll diameter, seam, support and access may force multiple shades or another treatment |

Lutron describes 1%, 3%, 5% and 10% solar-screen openness options and distinguishes translucent fabric from blackout fabric; this is a **selection vocabulary**, not a performance promise for an unspecified fabric. Show a real day/night sample, not a computer-generated opacity simulation. Sources: [Lutron fabric layering](https://www.lutron.com/us/en/articles/how-to-layer-window-treatments-with-motorized-shades), [roller shade types](https://www.lutron.com/us/en/window-treatments/shades/roller-shades), and [Lutron room-darkening design matrix](https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/3686848_luxury_resi_shade_matrix.pdf).

### Step 2 — Which treatment belongs in the room?

Roller shades are the primary architectural path for broad glazing, a crisp hem and a restrained sightline. Honeycomb/cellular can suit a smaller window or a different softness/insulation brief; Roman shades introduce folds and a textile presence; wood and horizontal-sheer blinds provide tilt or a different privacy/view behavior; drapery is appropriate when softness, acoustic character or larger fabric composition is the goal. Do not present all styles as interchangeable versions of a roller. Show a photographed room example and one explicit “choose this when” line for each treatment the DAVG team actually supplies. Lutron’s [residential window-treatment overview](https://www.lutron.com/us/en/window-treatments/residential-shades) documents the available treatment families; DAVG must verify which styles it will sell and support before publishing the complete gallery.

### Step 3 — Good / Better / Best fit guide (architectural ambition, not a quality verdict)

| Relative path | Likely fit | What changes beyond price | Power/design caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Good — professionally specified Triathlon / Triathlon Select** | Finished rooms and several coordinated windows where minimal construction and a clean roller result matter most | Battery or supported low-voltage variants, accessible fascia/open-roll choices, programmed scenes and credible retrofit flexibility | Select is a narrower configuration; its fabric and size choices must be checked against the actual window. It is not a drop-in replacement for a concealed dual roller |
| **Better — Sivoia QS, including the correctly sized roller chassis** | New build or renovation where hidden pockets, large windows, specialty mounting, precise alignment or a dual layer drive the design | Wider architectural configuration range; the correct Roller 64/100/150/300 or specialty product follows the measured opening and installation detail | Typically plan power, support, pocket and service access while construction is open; a **wireless control link** can still have **wired power** |
| **Best for visible craft — Palladiom wired or Wire-Free** | The shade mechanism is intentionally visible and must coordinate with architecture, finishes and Lutron wall controls | Designed exposed brackets, matched bottom rail and finish choices; wire-free version can achieve this look in a finished home | Palladiom is not automatically the right choice for a disappearing recessed pocket; each shade has its own drive and its geometry/fabric still sets a limit |

Lutron itself presents [Triathlon, Sivoia and Palladiom](https://www.lutron.com/us/en/window-treatments/residential-shades) as distinct professional design paths and its [technology/compatibility matrix](https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/3686848_luxury_resi_shade_matrix.pdf) separates battery power, wired power and wired/wireless communication. The “Good / Better / Best” labels are DAVG editorial shorthand for *fit and architectural ambition*, not a manufacturer quality grade or installed price quote. A Sivoia concealed roller may be the superior decision for a top-tier residence; a Palladiom Wire-Free shade may be the superior retrofit decision.

### Lutron shade-path comparison

| Shade path | Strongest fit | Architectural effect | Power/service consideration | Primary tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Triathlon / Triathlon Select | Finished homes and straightforward roller conditions | Low-profile visible top treatment or accessible open roll | Confirm exact model's battery or supported wired-power option and future access | Limited architectural configuration compared with engineered Sivoia pockets |
| Sivoia QS concealed roller | New construction and renovation with coordinated pockets | Shade can disappear into the architecture when raised | Confirm chassis, pocket, blocking, power/control route, service access and light gaps | Integration depends on early coordination and the correct enclosure size |
| Palladiom exposed roller, wired | High-design openings where visible hardware is desirable and wiring is available | Refined exposed bracket and bottom rail become part of the elevation | Coordinate support, finish, alignment, power route and access | Visible geometry and attachment must be resolved precisely |
| Palladiom Wire-Free exposed roller | Finished rooms needing the Palladiom exposed expression with less wiring work | Similar crafted visual intent without a concealed power route | Battery replacement access, RF design and exact size/fabric eligibility still matter | Less construction does not remove the need for blocking and alignment |
| Dual Sivoia roller / layered roller plus drapery | Openings needing both view-through solar control and room darkening/privacy | Two layers solve different daylight states | Larger assembly, separate drives and more control/trim coordination | Greater flexibility at higher spatial and installation complexity |

**Selection rule:** exposed versus concealed, battery versus hardwired, and wired versus wireless communication are three **separate** decisions. A beautifully executed exposed Palladiom shade can be more appropriate than a compromised recessed pocket, and a battery retrofit can be smarter than destructive wiring work. Never describe Sivoia QS as universally wired-communication only or Palladiom as invariably line-powered.

### Mounting, pocket and light-gap decisions

- **Inside mount:** preserves surrounding architecture but loses some usable fabric width to brackets and operator clearance; survey the opening at multiple points and show the expected side gap on a section.
- **Outside mount:** can overlap trim to improve coverage, but the shade and brackets become visible and may interfere with doors, handles or drapery.
- **Exposed roller:** an intentional design object when hardware/finish/attachment are resolved; it does not require a ceiling pocket, but still requires suitable structural support.
- **Fascia:** conceals the roller below an existing ceiling; evaluate its visible height, service access and proportion against the window.
- **Recessed pocket:** coordinate chassis, one/two rollers, roll diameter, hanger orientation, depth/height, blocking, wire entry and removable service access before framing. Lutron’s [measuring application note 598](https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/048598.pdf) gives, for example, Roller 64 **3.5 × 3.5 in minimum mounting space for a single shade** and **5.5 in height × 7.5 in depth for a dual mount**; these are configuration-specific minimums, **not** a universal finished pocket detail. Roller 100/150/300 and special conditions can require more room.
- **Room darkening:** a 0%-openness fabric can still allow light at side, top or bottom. Specify the actual side channel, bottom bar/sill and pocket condition when darkness matters. [Lutron’s design matrix](https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/3686848_luxury_resi_shade_matrix.pdf) states those details determine maximum room darkening.

### Power, communications and everyday control

“Wire-free,” “wireless,” and “battery” are not synonyms. Ask separately **how the motor receives power**, **how it receives commands**, and **how the owner acts**. A wired-power Sivoia QS may communicate via wired QS or a supported wireless receiver; Palladiom is available wired and Wire-Free; Triathlon includes battery and supported low-voltage variants. Show the real result: a wall button or supported keypad scene, scheduled position, app access when appropriate, and immediate manual override. Shade control may be included on an intentional shared lighting keypad; there is **no special wall slider** or extra shade keypad required by default. Check controller, receiver, shade and keypad compatibility in the chosen Lutron platform before promising an integration. [Lutron technology matrix](https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/3686848_luxury_resi_shade_matrix.pdf).

### Fabric decisions that materially change the result

| Decision | What the homeowner experiences | What must be tested or documented |
|---|---|---|
| Openness factor | Balance among view-through, glare reduction, daytime privacy, and solar control | Evaluate from the actual orientation and interior, not a loose sample alone |
| Color | Changes view, reflected light, exterior appearance, and perceived contrast | Review against glass, wall, trim, and daylight conditions |
| Blackout construction | Controls direct light but depends on side channels, sill, pocket, and room conditions for complete darkness | Define whether the goal is room darkening or near-total light control |
| Single versus dual fabric | One condition versus a flexible sheer/blackout combination | Confirm pocket depth, roll diameter, bracket, and control grouping |
| Railroaded or seamed fabric | Enables certain sizes but changes visual continuity | Approve seam placement and appearance before fabrication |

### What DAVG measures before specifying shades

- Finished and rough opening dimensions at multiple points.
- Window orientation, seasonal solar path, view priority, and nighttime privacy.
- Mullions, handles, trim, drapery, furniture, and door-operation conflicts.
- Pocket width/depth, blocking, power route, roll direction, fascia/reveal, and service access.
- Fabric roll diameter and alignment across adjacent openings.
- Grouping, speed/sequence expectations, local control, scenes, schedules, and sensor logic.
- Integration with **[architectural lighting scenes](/solutions/architectural-lighting/)** and the broader **[whole-home control architecture](/solutions/home-intelligence/)**.

### The cost of deciding late

Late shading decisions do not only add installation labor. They can reduce glass coverage, force visible fascia, expose wire, misalign adjacent shades, remove the dual-fabric option, or require a pocket that competes with structure and ceiling details. Early shade design protects the window architecture before it protects the technology budget.

## 09 — New build or finished-home installation

**New construction:** confirm pockets, structure, power, and window dimensions before framing and drywall.  
**Renovation:** coordinate with trim, ceiling, glazing, and electrical work while cavities are accessible.  
**Retrofit:** measure finished conditions and choose between battery, targeted wiring, fascia, or exposed architectural hardware.  
**Replacement:** document existing pockets, brackets, power, controls, and fabric widths before assuming new equipment will fit.

**Four construction questions that belong in the proposal:** (1) What framing, finish or electrical work is required per opening? (2) Who provides bracket blocking, line/low-voltage power and the final pocket dimensions? (3) Can an installer remove a shade for service without damaging finish? (4) When will the opening be field measured, and who signs off on fabric, seam and hembar alignment? A retrofit can be economical when a battery shade and appropriate exposed treatment fit; a new-build pocket can cost more in trade coordination while delivering a cleaner finished ceiling. Neither path has a universal price ranking.

## 10 — Prevent fit and mounting problems

- **No solid mounting structure:** issue blocking details before walls close.
- **Pocket sized by guess:** size from the selected shade, width, fabric roll, and configuration.
- **Misaligned hems across glass:** survey and group the elevation as one composition.
- **Wrong fabric for the objective:** evaluate view-through, glare, privacy, and darkening separately.
- **Visible wiring or inaccessible power:** coordinate routes and service access early.
- **Automation that surprises occupants:** make schedules legible and manual override immediate.

## 14 — Denver sun and homeowner questions

At elevation, strong solar exposure and large temperature swings make west- and south-facing glazing especially demanding. A Denver-area shade strategy should account for seasonal sun angle, mountain-view preservation, privacy after dark, and the way high-contrast daylight affects screens and interior materials.

### Motorized shading by location

Connect first to **[shading for Denver-area homes](/areas-served/)**. Phase-Two local pages should be created around documented window orientation, privacy, view, pocket, and fabric decisions—not generic city copy. Cherry Hills Village, Greenwood Village, Castle Pines, Castle Rock, and west/south-facing Denver enclaves are the strongest evidence-led candidates.

**What's the structural requirement for mounting shades on a large window wall?**  
The bracket, fastener and substrate must support the selected shade and its operating load. DAVG details blocking or an approved structural attachment with the architect/installer, particularly where finished drywall will conceal the support. A measured opening and the specific installation instructions control the answer.

**How much pocket depth do motorized shades actually need?**  
There is no universal depth. For illustration, Lutron’s [measuring guide](https://assets.lutron.com/a/documents/048598.pdf) identifies 3.5 × 3.5 inches as minimum mounting space for one Roller 64 assembly and 7.5 inches of depth for a particular dual Roller 64 assembly; that does not determine the finished pocket for another roller, fabric, fascia or service detail. Size the selected assembly on a section before framing.

**Are Lutron shades battery-powered or hardwired, and which is better?**  
Both exist. Battery models can reduce construction in a finished home but need service access and eventual replacement; wired-power models need a planned power path. Separately, controls may use a wired or wireless communication link. The opening, product family, desired hardware and construction stage decide the better fit.

**Do shades integrate with Lutron lighting scenes, or run independently?**  
Compatible shades and lighting can share a programmed Lutron scene and supported wall control—for example, lower a shade and dim a room for viewing. DAVG checks the selected platform, shade and keypad combination, preserves a local override, and explains what a schedule will do.

**What is the largest Lutron shade I can use in my home?**  
There is no single maximum for every product. The Section 06 matrix shows examples ranging from Triathlon Select up to 108 × 120 inches to larger specified Palladiom and Sivoia configurations. The fabric, width/drop pairing, area, seam, drive, bracket and mounting condition all have to pass the current model-specific sizing tool. Share an opening schedule for a real answer.

**Can a solar screen provide privacy at night?**  
Do not assume so. Openness, lighting on both sides of the glass and viewing angle change what is visible. Test a fabric at the real window and consider a translucent or blackout layer if evening privacy is required.

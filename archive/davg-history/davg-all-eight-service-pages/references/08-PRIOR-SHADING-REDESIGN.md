## Website copy — first reading layer

### 1. Opening

**Category:** Lutron motorized shades

**H1:** Daylight, shaped around the room.

**Deck:** Keep the view. Soften afternoon glare. Create privacy when the lights come on. DAVG designs Lutron roller shades around your windows, your rooms and the way you live.

**Action:** Plan Your Shading

The action opens or scrolls to the existing inquiry with shading preselected. Avoid another competing action next to it.

### 2. Start with what the room needs.

The fabric determines what you see, how light enters and how private the room feels. Start with the job of each window, then choose the motor and mounting detail.

**Keep the view.** Solar-screen fabrics soften daylight while retaining a view through the weave. Openness and fabric color affect the result. Compare samples at the actual window.

**Create privacy.** Translucent fabrics diffuse light and obscure the view. Check the chosen fabric with interior lights on; daytime appearance alone does not settle nighttime privacy.

**Darken the room.** Opaque fabrics block light through the material. Bedroom darkness also depends on the gaps around the shade and the mounting detail.

**Small explanatory line:** Openness describes the open area of the weave. It is not a percentage guarantee of glare reduction, heat rejection or privacy.

### 3. One window. Two different needs.

A living room may need a view during the day and privacy after dark. A bedroom may need filtered daylight and a separate blackout layer for sleep. Two independently controlled fabrics can serve those different moments.

**Day:** A solar layer filters daylight while keeping the exterior visible.

**Night:** A privacy or blackout layer closes the view into the room.

**Design note:** A dual roller needs room for both assemblies. A roller paired with drapery may suit the architecture better. We resolve the layers and mounting space together.

### 4. Disappear into the ceiling—or belong in the room.

**Recessed.** Coordinate a ceiling pocket so the raised shade is concealed. Resolve the roller, structure, power and service access before the ceiling is finished.

**Exposed.** Palladiom makes the bracket and bottom rail part of the design. Choose this route when the hardware should complement the window and interior finishes.

**Fascia.** A visible cover conceals the roller mechanism without requiring a recessed ceiling pocket. Its proportion and access still matter.

### 5. Three Lutron paths. Different reasons to choose them.

**Triathlon — a practical path for finished rooms.** Battery-powered configurations can add coordinated shades without a new power run. Supported wired-power configurations are also available. The fabric, top treatment and battery access still need to suit the opening.

**Sivoia QS — architectural flexibility.** Consider Sivoia when concealed pockets, layered rollers or demanding openings shape the design. The roller assembly follows the measured window, fabric and mounting condition. Power and control are coordinated with the building.

**Palladiom — hardware worth seeing.** Exposed brackets and a carefully resolved bottom rail bring a different architectural expression. Wired and Wire-Free versions offer different installation paths. Palladiom is a design choice, not an automatic upgrade over a concealed Sivoia installation.

| Family | Start here when | Resolve before ordering |
|---|---|---|
| Triathlon | Finished rooms and straightforward roller conditions | Fabric, fit, top treatment and power/access |
| Sivoia QS | Concealed or layered assemblies and more complex openings | Roller sizing, mounting, power/control and service access |
| Palladiom | Visible brackets and finish coordination are central | Bracket support, finish, geometry and wired/Wire-Free route |

**Small explanatory line:** Power and communication are separate decisions. A shade can receive wired power and still communicate wirelessly. The selected Lutron control system determines the compatible communication path.

### 6. Less daily adjustment. More useful control.

Operate one window, a room or a group of shades. Use a compatible keypad, remote or app, and set schedules around the household. Where supported, Lutron’s Natural Light Optimization can adjust shade positions around the sun’s movement.

**Morning:** Open the selected shades to bring daylight into the room.

**Afternoon:** Lower the appropriate solar shades to address glare at the seating or work area.

**Evening:** Close privacy shades as lighting scenes change.

For a home with Control4, we can coordinate compatible Lutron shading with broader household scenes. We confirm the processor, integration and intended behavior before specifying the system.

These are proposed scene examples, not a claim of a installed DAVG program. Show an ordinary selector; use truthful illustrative states, not an invented manufacturer dashboard.

### 7. The detail is decided before the finish.

A shade needs more than a space above the glass. It needs structural support, the correct clearance, a power path where required and a way to service the assembly later.

**Building or renovating?** Share the window elevations while ceilings and wiring are still being coordinated. That is the time to resolve pockets, dual rollers and concealed power.

**Updating a finished home?** Start with measured openings and photographs. Battery-powered shades, exposed hardware or fascia may provide a cleaner result with less construction.

### 9. Compare the scope—not just the shade.

The investment changes with the number and size of openings, fabric, one or two layers, hardware, power, construction and control. A useful proposal shows what each room receives and what work is included.

| Scope | What it delivers | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Good — focused shading | Solve a defined glare or privacy problem in selected rooms | Suitable fabric, measured fit and straightforward control |
| Better — coordinated rooms | Consistent shading across related rooms with useful groups and schedules | More openings, finish coordination and control planning |
| Best — architectural shading | Resolve shading with the ceiling, lighting and household operation | Selected pockets/exposed finishes, layering where justified and greater trade coordination |

These are DAVG scope descriptions, not Lutron grades or fixed-price packages. Any family can appear in more than one scope. A whole-home Triathlon project may cost more than a single Palladiom opening. Publish installed ranges only when DAVG has supplied current, scoped figures.

**Delivery:** Measure and sample → specify each opening → coordinate mounting and power → install and program → demonstrate controls and service access.

### 10. Questions worth answering

**Can shades be added without opening the walls?** Often. Battery-powered configurations can avoid a new power run, but mounting support, dimensions and access still need checking.

**Do solar shades provide privacy at night?** Do not assume so. When the room is brighter than outdoors, an open-weave fabric can allow a view inside. Choose and test a separate privacy solution where needed.

**Does blackout fabric make a bedroom completely dark?** It blocks light through the fabric. Edge gaps, the top detail and the bottom condition also determine the room’s darkness.

**Are wireless shades always battery powered?** No. Wireless describes the control link. Some shades use wired power with wireless communication.

**Which Lutron shade is best?** The best fit depends on the opening and architectural goal. Triathlon, Sivoia QS and Palladiom serve different installation and design priorities.

**Can they work with my existing controls?** We check the existing Lutron or automation system, processor and integration before confirming compatibility.

**Closing headline:** Start with your windows.

**Closing copy:** Tell us which rooms need better light or privacy, whether the home is finished or under construction, and what controls are already installed. Plans or window photographs help us define the next step.

**Form/action:** Plan Your Shading. Reuse the working form with project type, ZIP, rooms/priorities, existing control system, contact details and optional plans/photos. Do not add every technical selection as a required form field.

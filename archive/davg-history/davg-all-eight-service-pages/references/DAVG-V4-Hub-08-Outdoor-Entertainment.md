## 02 — What belongs in an outdoor system?

Outdoor entertainment combines weather-appropriate audio, video, lighting, connectivity, control, power, and cable infrastructure around the landscape and the way the property is used. DAVG designs the zones and boundaries first, then selects equipment that can perform in those real conditions.

The goal is even, comfortable coverage and simple control—not a television placed outdoors and a pair of speakers asked to cover the entire yard.

## 03 — How the property is used

Music can remain present across a patio at conversational level. The pool and dining areas can operate together or independently. A display can be visible from the intended seats without dominating the landscape. Lighting can support circulation, planting, architecture, and gatherings with distinct scenes.

One outdoor action can coordinate the relevant zones while indoor rooms continue doing something else.

## 04 — Zones, exposure and control architecture

The architecture joins property plan, use zones, speaker coverage and direction, acoustic boundaries, display orientation and brightness, landscape lighting circuits, Wi-Fi, control, trench and conduit paths, drainage, mounting, weather exposure, power, equipment location, and indoor-system integration.

Outdoor design must account for the full path from the protected equipment environment to every device on the property.

## 06 — Choose speakers, display and lighting

Resolve:

1. Which outdoor zones are used together and which need independence?

### Outdoor speaker-system comparison

| Approach | Coverage behavior | Visual effect | Best fit | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distributed landscape satellites plus subwoofer | Multiple nearby sources create more even level across listeners; low-frequency support is handled separately | Small satellites disappear into planting; subwoofer can be buried or integrated into landscape/hardscape depending on product | Larger patios, pools, gardens, and multi-zone properties | Using too few speakers at excessive volume |
| Surface-mount architectural speakers | Projects sound from walls, structures, or pergolas | Visible but can align with architecture and finish | Defined patios, covered terraces, and structures with useful mounting positions | Expecting one pair to cover a broad or irregular property evenly |
| Rock-style landscape speaker/subwoofer | Blends equipment into selected landscape conditions | Object is disguised rather than fully invisible | Landscapes where the form is appropriate and verified equipment meets performance needs | Scattering unrelated faux rocks without a coverage plan |
| Portable/wireless speaker | Moves with the user and requires little infrastructure | Fully visible consumer object | Occasional, small-area use | Treating temporary convenience as a whole-property audio design |

### The amplifier/cable decision inside an outdoor zone

Small defined areas may use low-impedance speakers with correctly matched amplifier channels and cable runs. A larger many-speaker landscape arrangement may justify a **70 V distributed line** with model-specific transformer taps and subwoofer/amplifier strategy; [Sonance landscape families](https://sonance.com/collections/sonance-landscape-series) include different configurations, not one electrical topology for every yard. Determine the listening area, speaker count, total tap/load, distance, independent volume zones and future service points before making the selection. Neighbors and property lines shape speaker orientation; more wattage is not a substitute for coverage design.

### Outdoor display decision table

| Location condition | Correct decision question | Planning consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Full shade | Does the display remain protected from direct sun, rain path, reflected glare, and extreme temperature? | Brightness may be less demanding, but outdoor rating, mount, power, and weather exposure still matter |
| Partial sun | When and from what direction does sun reach the screen or viewer? | Orientation, brightness class, anti-reflection behavior, shade strategy, and viewing time must be evaluated together |
| Direct sun exposure | Is a display specifically approved for that exposure and temperature condition? | Product class, heat, mount, cable, service, and cost change materially; an indoor display is not an acceptable substitute |
| Seasonal/occasional use | Will the display remain installed, covered, or removed? | Define protection, storage, connection, and re-commissioning rather than improvising each season |

Use the [manufacturer’s model-specific partial/full-sun installation guidance](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10006947/) as an illustration: The Terrace models differ in allowed sun exposure, and an IP rating alone does not settle direct sun, operating temperature, sealed connections or image readability. Record the exact model, mount location, sun path, viewing hours, glare, power and shelter in the display schedule. Avoid a universal nit or IP number.

### Why distributed sound is the luxury choice

The premium outcome is not maximum volume. It is consistent sound at conversational level where people sit, with deliberate reduction at the property edge. Distribution can improve both experience and neighbor consideration because no single loudspeaker is forced to throw across the entire yard.

## 09 — Install before or after hardscape

**New landscape or pool:** coordinate before hardscape, planting, irrigation, and concrete close the easiest pathways.  
**Renovation:** align work with patio, kitchen, pergola, pool, lighting, or landscape changes.  
**Retrofit:** map protected equipment locations and realistic cable routes; use trenching or targeted wireless only where appropriate.  
**Seasonal or system upgrade:** inspect water ingress, connections, mounts, cable, power, network, and equipment condition before replacing endpoints.

## 10 — Prevent loud corners and unsuitable displays

- **Two loud speakers covering the whole yard:** distribute sources closer to listeners and control zones independently.
- **Indoor display installed outdoors:** select from the actual weather and light exposure.
- **Outdoor Wi-Fi assumed to leak through the wall:** design exterior coverage as its own layer.
- **Conduit added after finished hardscape:** coordinate routes before landscape construction.
- **Sound projected beyond the intended area:** map property edges, orientation, and quiet zones.
- **Connections exposed to water and freeze-thaw cycles:** detail drainage, sealing, mounting, and service access.

## 12 — What changes outdoor investment?

Scope follows property area, zone count, speaker density, bass strategy, display size and exposure, lighting zones, trenching and hardscape access, mounting structures, outdoor network, equipment and amplifier capacity, power, weather protection, and integration.

Quote from a property plan and site walk. Generic patio packages obscure the boundary, infrastructure, and climate decisions that determine whether the result works.

**Relative scope:** a sheltered patio audio zone with existing routes differs from a multi-zone landscape network with buried satellite/sub cables; an outdoor display in an approved exposure adds mount, electrical, weather detail and sometimes a different model class; new hardscape/trench routes can dominate retrofit labor. Present equipment, property pathways, restoration, power/network, controls, commissioning and seasonal service separately. Use DAVG-verified installed ranges only after a site survey and design brief.

## 14 — Colorado exposure and late questions

Colorado outdoor systems face intense sun, UV exposure, rapid temperature change, snow, freeze-thaw cycles, and a strong indoor-outdoor living pattern. Equipment labels matter, but so do the mounting, drainage, cable, connection, and service details surrounding that equipment.

### Outdoor entertainment by location

Connect first to **[outdoor entertainment across the Denver area](/areas-served/)**. Phase-Two candidates should be driven by distinct patio, pool, landscape, exposure, and property-boundary conditions in Cherry Hills Village, Greenwood Village, Castle Pines, Castle Rock, Parker, and other areas where verified evidence exists.

**Can a TV actually survive outside in Colorado's climate?**  
Select an outdoor display whose **exact model** is rated for the actual partial/full sun, operating-temperature and weather exposure; provide a suitable mount, protected power/connections and service plan. An indoor TV generally should not be specified for unprotected outdoor installation. IP and brightness numbers vary by model and do not guarantee readability in a particular seat. [Samsung’s exposure guide](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10006947/) illustrates the partial/full-sun distinction.

**Can outdoor audio run as its own zone, separate from indoor?**  
Yes, when the audio/control architecture includes independent zones. A terrace may suit low-impedance loudspeakers; a larger multi-speaker landscape can justify 70 V distribution. The actual amplifier, transformer taps, cable and subwoofer design follow the layout and target listening levels, not property size alone.

**What's involved in adding AV to an existing patio or pool area?**  
Survey routes from protected equipment to each outdoor zone, including power, conduit, mounting, drainage, network signal and local electrical/pool constraints. Some patios permit targeted paths; others require trenching, hardscape restoration or a narrower scope. A wireless source or control still needs power and viable network coverage.

**Can lighting, audio, and video all run from one outdoor control point?**  
Compatible systems can be programmed into an outdoor scene from a suitable keypad or app. DAVG specifies the exact weather/location rating and supported controls, with direct access to volume, source and lighting so a single scene does not remove everyday adjustment.
